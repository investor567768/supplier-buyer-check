What it checks
  1. Who the company really is. A national register record bought from a Telegraph provider, plus the free GLEIF register. Name, number and country are compared with the listing.
  2. Sanctions. A paid sanctions screen. If the provider gives no answer, the check says so instead of calling the supplier clean.
  3. The website. Whether the company has its own site beyond the marketplace storefront, and whether that site is flagged as malicious.
  4. What the listing claims. Certifications, export markets and years in business are checked against independent web and news searches and the register. Each is marked found, not found or contradicted; nothing unproven is called true.
  5. Can they fulfil your order. Your quantity and deadline against the supplier's claimed capacity.
  6. Disputes. A paid news search for fraud, lawsuits and recalls.
  7. Side by side. Check several suppliers for the same order; the table ranks them by evidence.
  8. A second sanctions opinion. A second sanctions provider, run by a different builder, screens the same name. The supplier is only treated as clear when both agree.
  9. Every answer it bought. Register answers are compared with GLEIF and marked accepted or rejected, with the reason.
  10. The delivery. Before the balance is paid, the delivered report is checked against the register by Telegraph Evaluator.
  11. The invoice. Whether the account you are asked to pay belongs to the same company. A different account holder is the most common marketplace payment fraud.

It does not check beneficial ownership or product quality. Network: Telegraph testnet.
